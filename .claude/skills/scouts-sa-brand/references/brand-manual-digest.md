# SSA Brand Manual 2015, digest

Source: `BrandManual_SSA_2015.pdf`, 53 pages, Adobe InDesign, March 2015, from the SSA wiki (see `sources.md`). Produced with the advertising agency HAVAS pro bono. An updated version "which includes Meerkats" was listed as in development on the wiki as of 2026 but has not been published.

Section numbers below follow the manual.

## 1. Philosophy

- 1.1 Vision and mission as quoted in SKILL.md. WOSM vision: "As a global Movement, making a real contribution to Creating a Better World." WOSM mission: identical wording to the SSA mission.
- 1.2 Strengths: Involving, Exciting, Empowering. The manual lists "how" bullets for each:
  - Involving: engaging in activities; learning and teaching by doing; gaining personal commitment; creating opportunities to take part in building something; promoting diversity and equality and practising tolerance; mutual respect on an individual level.
  - Exciting: actively attracting people by offering new dimensions and exciting alternatives; evoking passion and commitment; initiating discoveries; experiencing friendship; leading and looking ahead.
  - Empowering: developing the social, physical, spiritual and leadership potential of the individual; enabling individuals and groups by establishing and practising democratic principles and structures; giving responsibility; conveying ethical values; strengthening personal and social maturity.
  - The strengths are to be used when defining the look (logo, ropes, colours, type, images), the character of communication (tonality, style, sender) and when selecting giveaways.

## 2. Design elements

### 2.1 World Scout Emblem
- Protected symbol, subject to licensing. Correct artwork comes from the World Scout Bureau.
- May not be used on its own in South Africa without prior written consent from WOSM through the National Scout Office.
- Only white on purple. Not on colour backgrounds. Not without the rope (corporate CI). Never distorted.
- Minimum size 6 mm high or 5 mm wide.

### 2.2 Logo
- Composition: WOSM emblem + wordmark "SCOUTS" + registration sign + country name. "SCOUTS" replaced the former usage of "World Organization of the Scout Movement".
- Neither the emblem, the wordmark nor the country name can be used separately.
- One single logo for the South Africa Scout brand; local regions must adopt it. The logo is never used for regional identification (Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, Mpumalanga, Eastern Cape examples shown as don'ts).
- Country name "South Africa" is capitalised because it is a title.
- Two design formats: corporate (page 7) and promotional badge form (page 34).
- Colour: white on purple. Exceptions: purple on white; white on black for non-colour applications (newspaper). Positive logo may appear black in photocopies. Never purple logo on dark backgrounds, never white logo on yellow, no other colours, no mixing.
- "Because this is a protected BRAND: never replace the World Scout emblem with your National Scout Organization emblem; never replace the word Scouts with another word."
- Size: minimum 40 mm wide with country name. Optimal widths: A2 100 mm, A3 64 mm, A4 64 mm, A5 50 mm, A6 40 mm (both orientations). Minimum with the claim 40 mm.
- Position: aligned to the right and left of the purple area closest to the page edge. Clear space equals the space to the edge of the page or purple area. Allow 8 mm non-printing margin for binding. Never centred as a single element of communication. Never distorted.
- Partner branding: SCOUTS honours partners' co-branding specs and expects the same. SCOUTS partner logo sits in a fixed-proportion purple rectangle (same for white/purple and black/white). Partner logo gets 2x clear space. Examples: "Member of the World Organisation of the Scout Movement", "SPONSORED BY", "Mpumalanga Provincial Scout Centre", "1st Nelspruit Scout Group". Minimum 40 mm. Do not use the purple logo in low contrast; a positive logo may be used against a dark background for contrast.

### 2.3 Rope and tag line
- The rope represents the colours of the South African flag and is the consistent secondary design element, together with the tag line "be prepared".
- Recognisable yet abstract; sections of the rope may be used. Never redesign, adapt or re-make the rope artwork; original artwork from the association.
- Versions: South African coloured, purple, black and white, white; also a tint of purple or transparent white.
- "be prepared" can be used with a portion or the whole rope; the sentence cannot be typed and used as a separate element. With the colour rope, "be prepared" is in black with four dots in the specified colours.
- Don'ts: colour rope on colour or purple backgrounds only sparingly; never overlap or scale ropes disproportionately.

### 2.4 Typefaces
- Futura: timelessly modern (1928). Used for the country name; Futura Bold Condensed for graphic elements, headlines and main titles of South African publications.
- Verdana: designed for on-screen display, generous width and spacing. Body text of all documents and all communications, website and emails.
- Specimen weights shown: Futura Bold Condensed, Futura, Verdana Bold, Verdana.
- (Promo CI, 5.2) AG Stencil: an extra graphic-element font to replace standard headlines as a fixed graphic device, always made to look stencilled onto the canvas, lower case only in the specimen.

### 2.5 Colours
- Eight-point checklist for brand colour: same shade and hue always; visible inside and outside; understood and used by all; on all communications; different from competitors; works worldwide; applied to more than the logo; comes with guidelines for partners.
- Purple meaning: wisdom, dignity, independence, creativity, mystery, magic; "almost 75% of pre-adolescent children prefer purple"; stability of blue plus energy of red; royalty, power, nobility, luxury, ambition.
- Primary: Purple Pantone 527 C/U/M, CMYK 79 94 0 0, RGB 98 37 153, #622599. White #FFFFFF.
- Pantone note: C, U and M suffixes are the same ink on coated, uncoated and matt paper; match all reproduction to Pantone as closely as possible, including textiles and foils.
- An additional colour may be used (for example regional identification) with significant contrast to the purple.
- Coding system, nine reserved colours (Pantone / CMYK / RGB / hex):
  - Black, Process Black, 0 0 0 100, 30 30 30, #1E1E1E
  - Blue, Reflex Blue, 100 82 0 2, 0 35 149, #002395
  - Green, 3415, 100 5 72 24, 0 122 77, #007A4D
  - Red, 179, 0 88 84 0, 222 56 49, #DE3831 (adults)
  - Yellow, 1235, 0 30 95 0, 255 182 18, #FFB612 (scouts)
  - Lime, 377, 51 5 98 23, 115 150 0, #739600 (cubs)
  - Sky blue, 2925, 84 21 0 0, 0 152 219, #0098DB
  - Orange, 021, 0 68 100 0, 255 88 0, #FF5800 (rovers)
  - Grey, 7544, 33 14 11 31, 137 150 160, #8996A0
- Colour spectrum wheel: lighter colours inner ring, darker outer; pink and the light tints flagged as "colours to avoid" and "colours to avoid in backgrounds".

### 2.6 Imagery
- Content over style. Goal: improve the quality of the message; concept simple enough for young people to produce photos themselves; rules apply at international, national, regional and group level.
- "We need balance and diversity": gender (girls and boys, women and men), age (youth movement, but inter-generational activities), cultures (the global movement is visible), activities (strengths and energy of young people), environment (conference room or outdoors, Scouts are active everywhere).
- Behaviour portrayed with purpose and credibility: young people in action in daily life, participating and reflecting social impact; avoid static, protocol-heavy, posed or staged images; "we always challenge stereotypes".
- People: individuals or groups present in the image; objects alone fail ("the building is nothing without the people inside").
- Scouts heritage: identifiable as Scouts (scarves, hats, uniforms). Non-Scout images with forced symbolic gestures are rejected.
- Style: natural colours and settings with wide perspective; no artificial colour, no removed backgrounds, no abnormal angles; real over posed.
- Activities map to strengths: Exciting (movement, action-packed) vs boring; Involving (working together, fully engaged) vs passive; Empowering (proud, with communities) vs controlling ("strict and controlled military formation is not conveying our true Scouting identity. Leadership should not be aggressive").
- Combinations portray diversity of cultures, gender and age; multi-cultural, multi-generational, boys and girls, "fun, with a purpose".

## 3. Corporate CI applications
- 3.1 Clothing: uniform badge set (South Africa brand badge, World Scout badge, Regional badge). Promotional clothing only manufactured by the National Office or with explicit written consent; bears the SSA logo without the rope; casual wear (hoodie, cap, T-shirt, buff).
- 3.2 Signage: templates apply; deviations approved by the National Office. Standard sign carries "Member of the World Organisation of the Scout Movement", Group name, Scout/Cub enquiry numbers, National Info Centre 0860 SCOUTS, www.scouts.org.za; variants with coloured rope, purple rope, directional arrows.
- 3.3 Flags: Group name plus section (Cubs, Scouts, Rovers).
- 3.4 and 3.5 Certificates: Wood Badge, Certificate of Merit, Star Awards; signed by National Commissioners, Chief Scout's Commissioner, Chief Scout.
- 3.6 PowerPoint: corporate template.
- 3.7 Letterheads: pre-printed purple header; National and Regional versions; "National or Province identification bar"; Groups get regional letterheads from their Region if in good standing, or use promo letterheads. Footer address block: PO Box 374, Newlands 7725, Tel +27 (0)21 685 8420, info@scouts.org.za, www.scouts.org.za.
- 3.8 Business cards: issued by National Office per Exco approval; black and white bar on the back identifies National or Region. Members use promotional cards (6.5).
- 3.9 Corporate CI for promotion: pull-up banner, booklet, car magnet, feather banner. Materials may not be altered without permission of the CEO.

## 4. Brand application
- 4.1 Principle of colour: categorisation by demographics (cubs, scouts, rovers, adults, plus "international"); logo clear space x with 2x to top edge; rope and frame aligned; white area is the flexible portion for photos following the imagery concept. New programmes reuse the existing colour code.
- Three levels of branded communication:
  - Maximum branded: strategy, youth programme, adult resources, governance, communication, management, policies.
  - Medium branded: international and national events, scout programmes, projects, periodicals, circulars.
  - Low branded: partnership advertising, regional publications, co-branding with NGOs.
  - Don'ts: the branded layout may not be more than one portion; the Scout logo must not sit outside the brand section.

## 5. Promotional CI design elements
- 5.1 Logo in badge form for all digital and most printed collateral; exceptions signage, banners, flags where the clean logo is more practical.
- 5.2 Backgrounds: canvas / burlap / hessian with a noticeable weave; neutral, purple (must match C79 M94 Y0 K0), hessian, white or cream variants; overlaps as frayed edges, straight lines sparingly; frayed ends, holes and tears as extras.
- Badges as design elements (flags in badge form, actual SSA badges). "No other design elements are permitted." The rope does not feature in the promo CI and may not be used.
- Typefaces as 2.4 plus AG Stencil.
- Imagery as 2.6 but style also matters: engaging, drawing the viewer in, "feel the energy and the passion, the challenges and the victories".

## 6. Promotional application examples
- Generic promotion ("Are you making a difference in your world?"), group promotion pamphlets by section (Cubs 7 to 10, Scouts 11 to 17, Rovers 18 to 30), adult recruitment ("Scouting is for Adults too!", "Get Involved, Get Excited, Be Empowered, Be Prepared!").
- PowerPoint: borders and SSA logo may not be cropped; no additional imagery; rope may not be used with the promotional CI.
- Certificates and invitations: no additional imagery nor the rope, except event logos and photographs.
- Quote used in collateral: Nelson Mandela on the Scout Movement as "a world leader in educating youth".
- Copyright line: "© Copyright 2013 SCOUTS South Africa, Registration number 019-215-NPO".

## Appendix
- A.1 Brand protection: the brand logo is the signature of World Scouting (emblem + "SCOUTS" + country name), an internationally protected registered trademark; "misusing it is an act of piracy"; conditions in WOSM Circular 5/07 and annexes "World Scouting's Brand and Its Trademarks". Queries to pr@scouts.org.za.
- A.2 Two corporate identities, never mixed in one document, presentation or promotional item:
  - Standard CI: everything not for promotional use. Marketing: flags, signage, clothing, specific merchandising. Administration: official letters, HR, contracts, financial, project and asset management, policies, reports, order forms. Volunteer management: membership documentation, indemnity forms, warrant certifications, event and award applications.
  - Promotional CI: brochures, pamphlets, posters, pull-up banners, PowerPoints, publications, adverts (print, online, window), announcements, newsletters, websites, car magnets, presentation covers, merchandising that does not need the clean CI; programme materials and training materials.

## 2020 infographic additions ("How is the SCOUTS SA brand used?")
- Three marks pictured: the World Scout brand (only WOSM may use it, unless written permission via the National Scout Office); the corporate SSA brand (National and Regional official communications); the old red national logo of the Scouting Association of South Africa (may not be used).
- Promo brand elements: textured backgrounds, SSA badges, "Be Prepared" tagline, Verdana and/or AG Stencil fonts, SSA logo in badge form.
- Offline: use or adapt the provided materials; make your own with the badge logo and the fonts; banners and stickers with the badge logo may be made by Groups; corporate-brand flags, banners and stickers come from the Scout shop; costly long-lived items like hall signage use the corporate brand templates.
- Online: Groups may add the SSA badge logo to websites and social media provided content, language and imagery are in line with SSA's ethos and policies; fonts are Verdana and/or AG Stencil; most images show the fun and adventure of Scouting rather than posed group shots; the site links back to the regional or national SSA site.
- Apparel: WOSM logo usage not encouraged (consent, item limits, royalties to the World Scout Bureau, DTI licence registration at the Group's cost). SSA badge logo in full colour allowed on non-purple Group apparel that is not sold for profit; no former SSA logos; lettering fonts Verdana, Futura, AG Stencil; items locally made. Corporate logo on non-purple garments only as a purchased membership or hiking cap badge sewn on, standing alone. Purple SSA apparel bought from the Scout shop may carry the Group name per the "Scout Groups going purple" rules (name never next to or on the same side as the SSA logo; Verdana).
- "The brand regulations are in place to protect members from having to pay royalties, license fees and/or fines." Usage is for PR, not commercial use.

## Marketing and Branding Policy v1/2016 additions
- SSA's brand is a WOSM "Country Identity Brand = National Scout Identity Brand", subject to WOSM guidelines and licensing (reviewed by WOSM in 2012); the licence is tied to South Africa's licence to run Scouting, hence "we are not permitted to use any older SSA logos".
- WOSM emblem: purple on white or white on purple only; NSOs may use it freely on educational, training and management material; everything else needs a licence; royalties on every item with the emblem or brand logo; pre-approval by the World Scout Shop; only NSOs may apply, so Groups apply through SSA and carry the admin costs.
- "SCOUTS South Africa" and variations are protected under the Heraldry Act 1962; Regions, Districts and Groups may use the Country Identity brand on websites, social media, letterheads etc. if they follow the manual.
- Merchandise: all SSA-branded merchandise for sale is manufactured only through the National Office; no merchandise using stand-alone advancement badge imagery; third parties need a contract.
- Advertising: local and community media need no consent; national, international and online media (excluding Twitter and Facebook) need 48 hours' notice to the National Office. No advertising for personal or corporate financial gain on SSA media or online platforms; third-party adverts need Marketing Committee or Exco permission.
- Communication: internal two-way communication encouraged; programme materials follow the brand (correct CI, imagery and tonality, vocabulary adapted to the audience; some Cub materials translated into vernacular languages). External communication portrays "a positive, innovative, adventurous and caring" image, carries the themes Social Force, Culture of Peace, Constructive Contribution, and the brand values Involving (constructive, educational, equal), Exciting (attractive, dynamic, innovative), Empowering (value-based, democratic, responsible). National, international and online media go through the National Office.
- Digital marketing: SSA recognises mobile-first access by youth in rural and impoverished areas; national and regional websites follow approved wireframes and the promotional brand; Groups are recommended to adopt the Group wireframes and promotional CI; social media must not be used for grievances or organisational queries; read with the Child Protection Policy and Social Media Guidelines.
- National Office postal address in 2016: PO Box 2434, Clareinch 7740 (the 2015 manual still shows PO Box 374, Newlands 7725).

## Letterhead guideline (Group and Event letterheads)
- Purple header band with the SCOUTS South Africa logo at the right and the Region name on the band; Group or event block always upper left; Group or event logo no bigger than 10 x 10 mm; Group or event name no bigger than 10 pt; contact lines 7 pt; typeface Verdana.
