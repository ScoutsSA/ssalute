# World Scouting (WOSM) brand: the 2024 rules and what they mean for SSA

Researched 2026-10-05 from the World Scouting Brand Centre at https://brand.scout.org, read in full through its public JSON API (all 74 pages of the "Scout Brand" guideline plus the Education Initiatives, Events and Campaigns guidelines, 122 pages in total), plus Circular GLB-C29-2024, the WOSM brand-refresh FAQ and the 2007 manual. SSA's 2015 manual was written against the 2007 World Scout brand; WOSM replaced that brand in August 2024 and SSA has not published a response.

## Brand framework (verbatim from the portal)

- **Vision**: "To be the world's most inspiring and inclusive youth movement creating transformative learning experiences for every young person, everywhere."
- **Impact statements**: A peaceful and inclusive world. A world shaped by youth. A sustainable world.
- **Strategic priorities, Movement**: Innovate education. Strengthen diversity and inclusion. Guarantee safeguarding and well-being. Value volunteering.
- **Strategic priorities, Organisation**: A fit for purpose organization. An adaptable organization. An influential organization.
- **Brand idea**: "Shaping the life you lead."
- **Value proposition**: "Equipping young people with the skills and values to shape the life they lead."
- **Tagline**: "Ready for life."
- **Experience principles**: Inclusivity ("We're a home away from home. A movement where everyone feels supported, safe and free."), Discovery ("Life's one big adventure. We make it fun, challenging and hands on, opening eyes and minds."), Creativity ("We champion creativity in everything we do, encouraging everyone to expand their skills."), Impact ("We proudly promote the positive change and impact we all create, and seek to make.").
- **Manifesto ("Our story")**: "We're all different. We all have unique quirks and characters, interests and aptitudes. And the experiences gained in Scouting, will shape the life you lead, the communities you build and the world you'll create. Be prepared, as we say. Because through our global movement we set young people on a journey of self-discovery, enriching and equipping them with a sense of adventure, curiosity and appreciation for different cultures, opening eyes, minds and hearts to whatever might lie ahead. It is an experience like no other, helping form the foundational skills and values for all walks of life imaginable: inventors, adventurers, artists, teachers, athletes, scientists, entrepreneurs. Whatever your passion, whatever your calling, whatever your path, this is about Shaping the life you lead."
- Figures used in the guideline: "60 million Scouts active through 176 National Scout Organizations"; the website mock-up shows 57 million members, 2.3 billion hours of community service, 16 million service projects, 172 NSOs; the IVY campaign page says "more than 60 million members ... including 4.5 million adult volunteers".

## Master World Scout Emblem

- Called the Master World Scout Emblem. Dates to Brownsea Island; redesigned 2024 to match the WOSM Constitution description. Symbolism: the fleur-de-lys is the compass and embodies the values, the two stars stand for truth and knowledge, the bond brings everyone together, the rope symbolises unity as a global family.
- "A unique asset shared with our National Scout Organizations and adorns the first badge all new Scouts receive."
- Colourway: **Scouting Purple and Canvas White across all physical and digital applications**. A reversed version exists "for exceptional use only".
- Supplied as rasterised files for print and digital. "Always use the artwork available. Never reproduce or adapt the Master World Scout Emblem." Trademark and copyright registered.
- Clear space is built into every file; nothing may enter it and it may never be cropped. **Minimum height 5 mm physical, 50 px digital.**
- Two-layer artwork (rope plus fleur-de-lys on one layer, stars see-through to the purple field) that "must not be altered".
- Where it is used: "key experiences", meaning hero moments, universal experiences, or as a "watermark of authenticity": World Scout Badge, World Scout Flag, World Scout event logos, official certificates and awards, installations and murals. It may be added to World Scouting logo applications as an authenticating element on corporate materials (letterheads, heritage articles). Combining emblem and logo is otherwise "controlled by the brand team".
- Physical finishes (sewn, foil, spot UV, deboss) are encouraged for premium feel.
- Don'ts: off-brand colours; changing or swapping elements; adjusting proportions or rotation; below minimum size or cropped; drop shadows or glows; inappropriate comms; detaching an element from its layer.

## World Scouting logo

- The lead element "in all instances where the World Scouting brand is being represented". Logomark (simplified emblem, "harks back to the very first badge created by Baden-Powell, before the stars and the distinctive use of purple were added") plus the "World Scouting" wordmark in a fixed relationship. English and French versions only.
- **NSO permission, verbatim**: "The World Scouting logo can be used by National Scout Organizations to signify and visualise their status as Member Organizations of the World Organization of the Scout Movement."
- Colourways: Scouting Purple on the lighter palette and bright photography; Canvas White on the darker palette and imagery. The fleur-de-lys is see-through: "Do not fill in, always show the background through it."
- **Clear space 33% of logo height** (built into files). **Minimum height 10 mm physical, 35 px digital.**
- Placement: left side, top or bottom corner, for stationery and literature covers; bottom corner as a sign-off for presentations, advertising and posters; right side allowed for right-to-left languages; centred only when the logo is the sole element (business card back, video animation). **Logo height A3 20 mm, A4 14 mm, A5 12 mm**, scaled proportionately above A3.
- Don'ts: the same seven as the emblem, plus "do not fill in the fleur-de-lys".

## Logo shorthand (logomark alone)

- Used where the full logo does not fit or is too formal: circular social avatars, favicons, small applications, merchandise labels, apparel.
- Scouting Purple or Canvas White "the majority of the time"; other palette colours only by exception, approved by the brand team, and generally with a purple version alongside.
- "Discovery window" treatment (Reveal: imagery or stitches inside the mark; Focus: magnify; React: swap imagery or reverse stitch colours) is a minimal-use style and "should not be used with the World Scouting logo".
- Clear space 33% of height, minimum 10 mm / 35 px; clear-space rules do not apply to avatars and favicons.

## Tagline "Ready for life"

- Set in Scouts GT Planar with bespoke letterforms for Latin scripts; a custom mark with "flat geometry and rounded corners" may be created for other scripts. Artwork in English, French, Arabic and Spanish.
- Use "with care and sparingly": poster campaigns, key merchandise, new-employee presentations, the Scouts story, an about-us slide.
- Adaptive form: "Ready for [emotive word]" (examples on the portal: ideas, fun). The inserted word must be emotive, set in a typeface that is "not too similar" to Scouts GT Planar, and visually balanced with the fixed letterforms.
- **Clear space 66% of tagline height; minimum 8 mm physical, 35 px digital.**
- Don'ts: never lock up with the World Scouting logo; no off-brand colours; never recreate in another typeface; no proportion or rotation changes; not below minimum size; no effects; not on inappropriate comms; adaptive words never in Scouts GT Planar and never non-emotive.

Rules:
- Always start with one or both primaries. **Maximum four colours per application, including at least one primary.** Dark purples read as serious; pink, green and white as bright.
- Pantone for spot, CMYK for four-colour, RGB for digital, hex for web, textile references for fabric.
- **WCAG pairings** (each colour tested as text or background against the three base colours):
  - On or with Scouting Purple: Canvas White, Ember Orange, River Blue, Leaf Green, Blossom Pink (all AAA).
  - On or with Canvas White: Scouting Purple (AAA), Midnight Purple (AAA), Forest Green (AAA), Fire Red (AA), Ocean Blue (AA).
  - On or with Midnight Purple: Canvas White, Ember Orange, River Blue, Leaf Green, Blossom Pink (all AAA).
- "No other colours should be used for body copy." Body copy is Midnight Purple on Canvas White, or Midnight Purple / Canvas White on colour depending on legibility.
- Don'ts: more than four colours; a combination without a primary; illegible copy pairings; off-brand colours.

## Typography

- **Scouts GT Planar** (also written "WS GT Planar"): the hero typeface, a bespoke cut of Grilli Type's GT Planar in one weight (Bold) with two italics, Italic 15 and Retalic 15. For titles, hero statements, quotes, key data, large graphics, website headers, signage, poster titles, merchandise, stationery. Set in **Bold, sentence case, -20 tracking**; "words should be close but never colliding". Italics highlight keywords, no more than three words per sentence. Other weights are available from Grilli Type.
- **Noto Sans**: the secondary typeface, from Google Fonts, 800+ languages. Regular for body copy, graph labels, footnotes; ExtraBold for titles, subheads, standfirsts, highlights; **-10 tracking, sentence case**. Other weights only when legibility or large sizes require it.
- **Office and internal applications** (reports, PowerPoint, letters, email) use Noto Sans for everything, titles included.
- Colour for type: Scouting Purple, Canvas White and Midnight Purple are the main type colours; secondary colours for pace and highlights, three ways: a core colour, a tonal pairing (Ocean Blue with River Blue), or a contrasting combination.
- "Making our type unique": integrated type (words following elements in an image, single words or statements under ten words) and custom letterforms built from the shapes and stitches (up to four words). Hero moments only, never low-level information.
- Don'ts: off-brand typeface; off-brand leading or tracking; illegible colour pairings; off-brand colours; off-brand type styles; mixing weights or case.

## Graphic language

- **Shapes**: three core shapes derived from the emblem's structure and Scout badges; cropping them yields more. Three behaviours: "Discover the world" (a window onto imagery, pattern, colour or words), "Building new experiences" (combine shapes into ownable icons: map pin, signage, stat, smiley), "Creating bespoke connections" (shapes plus a creative inspiration, reserved for events and campaigns and agreed with the brand team; examples start from location characteristics or subject matter). Don'ts: off-brand colours; adding shapes beyond the core three; changing proportions or rotation; complex illustrations; effects; cluttered applications.
- **Stitches**: six stitch marks from the badge's woven construction, supplied as vector and image files, **4 pt stroke on formats below HD widescreen (digital) or A3 (print)**, scaled visually with the format; live vectors from the brand team. Any palette colour, bold or tonal pairings. Three behaviours: "Stitched together" (linking content, including across pages and signage), "Structured detail" (structure, highlight, direction), "Rich tapestry" (large decorative pattern, integration into images, words or shapes; physical stitching allowed). Don'ts: off-brand colours; extra stitch patterns; erratic form; undefinable sizing; effects; interrupting the message.

## Imagery

- Two categories: "people and activities" (reportage, accurate representation of diverse people, activities and events, conveying action, energy and scale) and "atmospheric details" (close, almost abstract moments: condensation on a tent, rope threads, a footprint in dried mud), often found by cropping.
- What to shoot: represent true diversity (all ages, genders, cultures); capture what we see ("show the scuffs, mud, lens flares and the out of focus moments ... do not ask people to pose"); show all sides of Scouting (conference, countryside, community, not clichés).
- How to use: crop to make space, crop in, or zoom out; hero moments full bleed, held in a shape, or layered; supporting detail as functional figures.

## Tone of voice

- Core voice: "inspiring youth". "If you remember nothing else, try and write with this in mind."
- Voice principles map to the experience principles and need not be used equally:
  - **Inclusivity, "We welcome everyone!"**: inclusive language that breaks down barriers; accessible grammar and everyday English, no jargon, **sentences under ten words**; empathy and offers of support.
  - **Discovery, "We're naturally curious"**: open-ended questions; exploratory language; invitations to take part; vivid detail; surprising facts.
  - **Creativity, "We know when to be playful"**: idioms in moderation; alliteration and rhyme; exclamation marks sparingly; varied sentence rhythm ("Short sentences can really work. Try it."); humour in moderation; a mix of formal and informal.
  - **Impact, "We lead on the big issues"**: be informed; urgency where appropriate; show how people can get involved.

## Sample applications

Image-only pages for clothing, accessories, advertising, signage, murals, brochures, posters, office stationery (letterhead, envelopes, business cards, email signature, embossed emblem, labels), social media, website, presentation (standard and enhanced PowerPoint), virtual backgrounds. The website mock-up uses Scouting Purple hero panels with a large shape, white Scouts GT Planar headings, stats in secondary colours, photos cropped into shapes, Noto Sans body. No component library, grid or spacing tokens are published.

## Education initiatives, events and campaigns (NSO-relevant bits)

- Each initiative guideline says it exists "to help National Scout Organizations effectively integrate [the initiative] into their national youth programmes". Initiative logos (Messengers of Peace, EarthTribe, HealthAllies, LifeLeaders) "can be used by National Scout Organizations to communicate and promote the education initiative in their country", always from supplied artwork, never reproduced. Messengers of Peace icon minimum width 2.5 cm because it contains the emblem. The Scouts for SDGs mark may be used to mobilise young people.
- Regional Scout Conference logos include the Master World Scout Emblem "placed with clear prominence" and come in full colour, white and black.
- JOTA-JOTI has its own logo (a "J" built from the brand shapes), adds Amber and Vermilion to the palette, and the portal suggests how NSOs create national or local JOTA-JOTI badges, with a working file.
- IVY 2026: NSOs may adapt or use the WOSM bilingual visuals and Scouts del Ecuador's badge in national campaigns; hashtags #VolunteerYear and #IYY2026.

## What NSOs may and may not do (confirmed)

- Use the Master World Scout Emblem as the World Scout Badge on uniforms and as a symbol of belonging at events and celebrations; NSOs decide when to adopt the redesigned emblem on uniforms.
- Use the World Scouting logo to show Member Organization status; encouraged, with a flexible transition period.
- Combine emblem and logo only in brand-team-controlled applications.
- Non-commercial educational, training and management use by an NSO is free; products need a non-commercial licence; anything sold needs a commercial licence (historically 5% royalty via the World Scout Shop). WOSM is reviewing all NSO licences after August 2024.
- **Not published**: a 2024 template for national "SCOUTS + country" lockups, "Member of WOSM" wording, or a rule for placing a national logo beside the World Scouting logo. The 2007 manual's country-identity annex (written agreement with the World Scout Bureau; country name inserted into the brand logo "as presented through the example of South Africa"; never replace the emblem with an NSO emblem; never replace or translate "Scouts") remains the only published NSO lockup rule and it describes the SSA logo exactly.

## What this means for ssalute and the support bot

1. **Nothing in our colour tokens changes.** `#622599` is correct under both brands. The 2007 secondary "coding system" colours (SSA's nine) are retired at WOSM but still SSA's; keep using them only as SSA section colours.
2. **Keep following the SSA manual for the SSA logo.** SSA's lockup is a WOSM-licensed Country Identity Brand under the 2007 model. Do not invent a "World Scouting" style version. Both apps are National Office systems (the product owner is SSA's National Chair for IT and sits on ManCo), so showing membership is within SSA's own NSO permissions: the published route is the World Scouting logo (English version) at 35 px or more with 33% clear space, from supplied artwork, never locked up with "Ready for life". Treat adding it as a ManCo brand decision, not a UI ticket.
3. **Never use "Creating a Better World" in new copy.** "Ready for life" is WOSM's tagline and never locks up with any logo; SSA's own tagline "be prepared" is unaffected (WOSM's manifesto even quotes it).
4. **WOSM's voice principles are a good tie-breaker for SSA copy**: sentences under ten words, no jargon, curiosity, playful but measured, exclamation marks sparingly, always show how to get involved. They sit comfortably inside SSA's Involving, Exciting, Empowering.
5. **Accessibility pairings are reusable** because SSA shares Scouting Purple: white, Ember Orange, River Blue, Leaf Green and Blossom Pink all reach AAA on `#622599`. Anything in SSA's own section palette (lime, yellow, orange, red) on purple is not WOSM-tested; check contrast yourself.
6. **Noto Sans is an acceptable on-brand fallback** where Verdana is wrong for the medium and Instrument Sans is unavailable. Midnight Purple `#4D006E` is WOSM's body-text colour, not SSA's; do not adopt it for SSA body text. The 15% tints are WOSM's; SSA's equivalent is the purple-tint token.
7. **Facts for copy**: 60 million Scouts in 176 NSOs (World Scouting, 2024), not SSA's 2015 "32 million".
8. When WOSM publishes NSO lockup guidance or SSA publishes a new manual, revisit this file first.

## History relevant to SSA

- 24 February 2008: the South African Scout Association unveiled the World Scout brand as its national brand, first NSO to do so, and became "SCOUTS South Africa".
- 2012: WOSM reviewed its licensing; SSA's 2016 policy ties the brand to South Africa's licence to run Scouting, which is why older SSA logos are forbidden.
- March 2015: SSA Brand Manual (HAVAS) refined the brand and added the promotional CI.
- September 2016: SSA Marketing and Branding Policy v1/2016.
- May 2024: World Scout Committee approved the refresh (agency Dragon Rouge); 5 August 2024: Circular GLB-C29-2024 and launch; 43rd World Scout Conference, Egypt, August 2024: in force. Portal pages last revised 28 August 2025; palette updated 2 September 2026.
